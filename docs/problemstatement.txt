ROBLEM STATEMENT 3
Pay the right bill, to the right person
Sixty invoices a week, three scripts, one banking portal and one tired accountant. Get the
right vendors paid on time, and refuse to pay when something is off.
THE SCENE (ILLUSTRATIVE)
Raghu runs a hardware wholesale in Coimbatore. His vendors invoice in Tamil, Hindi and English,
some printed, some handwritten on delivery slips with bank details scribbled underneath. Every
Friday his accountant retypes the week's invoices into a banking portal. Last month one vendor's
invoice arrived with a new account number. It looked fine. It was not the vendor's.
THE CHALLENGE
Build an agent that reads a pile of multilingual invoices and gets the right vendors
paid the right amounts in the right order, and that knows when to stop and say no.
WHAT MAKES IT HARD
• Invoices do not agree with themselves. Totals, tax and line items drift, and amounts appear in
figures and in words, sometimes in lakh notation and regional scripts.
• The same invoice returns in disguise. A new number, a different date format, a slightly different
spelling.
• A familiar vendor with new bank details is the oldest trick there is.
• Time carries legal weight. Registered micro and small suppliers must be paid within 15 days (no
written agreement) or 45 days (with one) for the buyer to claim the expense that year. Cash is limited,
so the question is also what to pay first.
• Money that has left cannot be un-sent. Retries, failures and bank reversals must never become
double payments.
THE MOMENT WE WANT TO SEE
A doctored invoice goes in. The agent declines to pay it, tells the owner why in his own language,
while the clean invoices go out and come back confirmed.
RED LINES
• Money never moves to an account that has not been verified as belonging to the claimed vendor.
• Approval cannot be spoofed, replayed or given by accident.
• A retry must never become a second payment.
• Every payment traces back to its invoice, its checks and its approver.
QUESTIONS YOUR DESIGN MUST ANSWER
? When should the agent pay on its own, and when must it stop and ask?
? How should it treat a familiar vendor whose bank details have changed?
? With limited cash and deadlines that carry tax consequences, what gets paid first?
? What happens when a payout fails, or is reversed after it looked successful?

WHAT EACH LEVEL LOOKS LIKE
SOLID Reads a batch of invoices, verifies one vendor's account, and completes a sandbox payout.
STRONG Catches duplicates, arithmetic that does not add up and changed bank details, with the owner
approving in his own language.
EXCEPTIONAL Decides the order of payment against cash and deadlines, and handles failure and reversal
without ever paying twice.
